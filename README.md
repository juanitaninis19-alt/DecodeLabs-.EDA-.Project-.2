## Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) | Project 2

### DecodeLabs Data Analytics Internship

## About This Project

This is my second project as part of the DecodeLabs Data Analytics Internship.

After cleaning and preparing the sales dataset in Project 1, I moved on to exploring the data to understand what the numbers actually reveal about sales performance and customer purchasing patterns.

For this project, I worked with **1,200 sales transactions** using Microsoft Excel. My goal was not just to calculate figures or create charts, but to understand the patterns behind the data and identify useful insights that could support business decisions.

## Project Objectives

I focused on:

- Understanding the overall sales performance.
- Identifying trends in monthly revenue.
- Comparing product performance and payment methods.
- Examining how transaction values are distributed.
- Detecting unusual transactions.
- Exploring the relationship between quantity purchased and total order value.

## Tools and Techniques Used

**Tool:** Microsoft Excel

**Techniques:**
- Descriptive statistics using Excel formulas.
- PivotTables for summarizing sales data.
- Bar, column, and line charts for comparing sales performance.
- Histogram for examining order value distribution.
- Interquartile Range (IQR) for identifying potential outliers.
- Pearson correlation and scatter plot for examining relationships between variables.

## Key Findings

Here are some of the insights I discovered during the analysis:

**1. Overall Sales Performance**

The dataset contained 1,200 transactions, generating a total revenue of **1,264,761.96**.

The average order value was **1,053.97**, while the median was **823.62**, showing that some higher-value transactions increased the overall average.

**2. Monthly Revenue Trend**

January recorded the highest revenue, while December recorded the lowest.

The monthly revenue chart showed a declining trend across the period analyzed. This is an area worth investigating further to understand what contributed to the changes in sales.

**3. Product Performance**

Chair generated the highest total revenue among the products analyzed.

This finding could be useful when reviewing inventory decisions and identifying products that contribute significantly to sales.

**4. Payment Methods**

Credit Card was the most frequently used payment method, accounting for **19.50%** of all transactions.

This also highlighted the importance of maintaining convenient payment options for customers.

**5. Sales Distribution and Outliers**

The distribution of order values was positively skewed, meaning lower-value transactions occurred more frequently than higher-value ones.

Using the IQR method, I identified **8 potential outliers**, representing approximately **0.67%** of all transactions.

These values were flagged for further review rather than automatically removed.

**6. Correlation Analysis**

The correlation between Quantity and TotalPrice was **0.615**, indicating a moderate positive relationship.

This suggests that orders containing more items generally tend to have higher total values, although other factors, such as product prices, also influence the amount spent.
